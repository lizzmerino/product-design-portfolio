Case Study

Discount Tool

RoleSenior Product Designer
Year2023
TypeFeature Design · Fintech

Making early payment discounts feel intuitive, not like a spreadsheet.

Bancoli's core value proposition for international B2B trade is dynamic discounting: sellers can incentivize buyers to pay invoices ahead of schedule in exchange for a discount. The challenge was making that mechanic feel natural in the flow of creating an invoice, without requiring users to understand the underlying financial logic.

This was designed almost from scratch. No existing pattern in invoicing tools handles date-based discount calculation quite this way. Most tools offer a simple percentage field. Our approach ties the discount directly to timing, and the interface needed to make that relationship immediately legible to a seller who just wants to get paid.

01 · Problem

A concept that didn't survive the form

The original discount entry was a pair of input fields: a percentage and a date. It was technically functional, but fell apart in internal testing. Users struggled to reason about what number to enter. The discount wasn't inherently about a percentage; it was about giving the buyer more time to pay early and rewarding them accordingly. The form abstracted that relationship away entirely.

The mental model users needed was: "if my buyer pays this many days before the due date, the system calculates what discount makes sense." Free-form percentage input forced sellers to do that math themselves, or guess. Both led to errors, inconsistency, and lack of confidence in the feature.

"The discount isn't a percentage you decide. It's a relationship between timing and value. The design had to make that visible."

Before: two empty fields. Sellers had to guess both the percentage and the date.

02 · Approach

Auto-calculate first, give control second

The redesign started by flipping the model: instead of asking sellers to input a discount percentage, the system calculates it automatically. When a seller sets an invoice issue date and due date, Bancoli computes a suggested early-payment discount, displayed immediately in the sidebar with a friendly prompt ("I suggest a discount of 1.97%"). No manual entry required for the default case.

1Set the due date2Turn on discount3Bancoli suggests the %

But during internal testing, even with auto-calculation, users felt a loss of control. They wanted to understand how the number was generated and be able to adjust it. That tension led to the discount modal: a dedicated interface where the seller can see the relationship between days-early and discount percentage, with two sliders to manipulate both.

The top slider sets how many days early the buyer can pay. The system then calculates the corresponding discount percentage, anchored around a base rate of approximately 2%, and shows it on the second slider. Sellers can leave both at the suggested values or adjust them. The deadline date updates live as they move the slider, giving immediate temporal feedback.

1Edit the suggestion2Move the days, % follows3Fine-tune the %4Apply

03 · Outcome

Shipped as designed. Still running.

The feature launched and was adopted without friction. No significant support tickets related to the discount flow were raised post-launch, a meaningful signal for a financial feature with this level of conceptual novelty. The auto-suggestion handles the default case for most users; the modal gives power users the granularity they want.

Three years on, the discount tool remains one of Bancoli's differentiating features in the B2B invoicing space. The slider approach turned out to be the right abstraction: it makes the timing-value relationship visible without requiring users to understand the math underneath it.
