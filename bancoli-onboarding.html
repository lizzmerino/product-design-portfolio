KYC Onboarding

RoleSenior Product Designer
Year2022 – Present
TypeFintech · B2B · Compliance
Context

A fintech platform where onboarding is the only gate to revenue.

Bancoli is a B2B fintech platform built for international trade finance — serving small businesses and solopreneurs who need to move money across borders. I joined as Senior Product Designer and owned the end-to-end UX of the onboarding experience.

The Problem

30+ required steps. No way to shorten them.

To access Bancoli's financial services, every new user had to complete a KYC process before doing anything in the product. The flow covered business classification, company details, member information, identity verification, and document submission — over 30 steps in total.

The original implementation was a long-form experience. Users saw everything at once, had no sense of progress, and frequently dropped off before completing. For a product where onboarding is the only gate to revenue, that drop-off was a critical business problem.

"The challenge wasn't what to ask — it was how to ask it."

The Constraint

Every field existed because compliance required it.

None of those 30+ steps could be removed. Working within a heavily regulated environment meant the design solution had to live entirely in the interaction layer — not in reducing scope. Any proposal had to be signed off by the compliance team before it could ship.

The Approach

One question at a time.

Inspired by conversational UI patterns, the core idea was simple: instead of presenting the entire form at once, the interface would scroll to the next question automatically after each answer — keeping the user focused and the experience feeling fast even when it wasn't.

The flow was broken into clear chapters — business type, company information, member details, identity verification, and document upload — so users always knew where they were and what was coming. Users who self-identified as solopreneurs were routed through a shorter version of the company section, reducing unnecessary friction for that segment. The full experience was also designed for mobile, where the single-question pattern proved especially effective on smaller screens.

To reinforce a sense of forward momentum, we added a persistent progress bar throughout the flow — giving users a clear visual anchor of how far they'd come and what remained. The team also introduced a rewards mechanic that offered users incentives for completing each chapter, turning a compliance requirement into something closer to a goal-driven experience. Completion rates responded accordingly.
