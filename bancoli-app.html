Case Study

Block System

RoleSenior Product Designer
Year2022 – Present
TypeFintech · Design System · Web App
Context

Four years of designing one app.

I designed the Bancoli web app end to end: dashboard, invoicing, bill pay, smart accounts, Web3 wallets, contacts and settings. Over four years the product kept growing, and every new feature asked for a new screen.

This case focuses on the latest and most important step in that evolution: a block system that turned the whole app into a set of reusable pieces.

The Problem

Every section was its own layout.

Each time we shipped a feature, it got a custom page. Cards had different paddings, buttons lived in different places, and the dashboard slowly filled with one-off components. Designing a new section meant starting from zero, and engineering had to rebuild similar pieces again and again.

"Instead of designing pages, we started designing blocks, and let pages be arrangements of them."

The Approach

A small library of blocks.

I audited every screen and grouped what repeated into a handful of block types: account balance, stats, actions, promos, banners, account rows and tables. Each block follows the same rules: one corner radius, one padding, one primary action, and only two widths, half or full.

Blocks have slots, so the same block can show a Smart USD balance, Reward Points or a Merchant account without becoming a new component.

How it works

Same blocks, different sections.

Building a section became a matter of choosing blocks and arranging them. The dashboard leads with the balance and a promo; Smart account swaps the promo for stats and adds the account cards; Contacts opens with a banner; Invoicing puts stats and an action on top of the invoice table.

The sidebar also moved to a light, baby blue version so the blocks, not the navigation, carry the page.
